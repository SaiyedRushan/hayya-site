      How it works

      A call you can't ignore

      You don't ignore a phone call. Hayya makes salah just as hard to walk past.

      1. 1

        It rings like a phone call

        Full screen, with its own ringtone. On iOS 26 it rings like a Clock alarm, even when your phone is on silent. On Android it covers the lock screen.

      2. 2

        It keeps calling until the prayer's time ends

        Leave it and it hangs up, then rings again a few minutes later. You can snooze it once. If the time for that prayer runs out, it's logged as missed and your streak starts again.

      3. 3

        Declining takes more than a tap

        Pick a reason or write your own. Hayya keeps what you wrote and shows it to you later. Or have it call you back in 20 to 60 minutes instead.

      4. 4

        Fajr can ask you to say the duʿā out loud

        Turn this on and the Fajr alarm keeps ringing until you say the waking-up duʿā aloud. It's the surest way to be properly awake.

      5. 5

        Prefer a quiet reminder? You can have one

        Switch any prayer to a notification: one banner, no ringing, no calling back. Add your own alarms too, like Tahajjud or "20 minutes before Fajr".

      New in version 1.3.0

      Hayya on your watch

      Including an Islamic watch face with Arabic numerals for Wear OS. Your watch takes your city and settings from your phone and works out the times itself, so they stay right when your phone is in another room.

      Apple Watch

      watchOS 10 or later. It installs with Hayya on your iPhone.

      Wear OS

      Wear OS 3 or later, like Galaxy Watch and Pixel Watch. Install Hayya from the Play Store on your watch.

      When a prayer calls, your watch rings too. Snooze it from your wrist. Answer and Decline open Hayya on your phone, where the prayer is logged.

      An Islamic watch face, made for prayer times

      Watch faces with Arabic numerals or Islamic patterns are hard to find. Hayya's comes with Arabic numerals (١٢ ٣ ٦ ٩), an eight-pointed star like the ones in Islamic geometric art, a classic dial, analog or digital, in 12 colours. The next prayer and the time left until it are always on the dial, with two spaces you fill yourself, like battery or the date.

      What is Hayya?

      Hayya is a free Muslim prayer times app for iPhone, Android, Apple Watch and Wear OS. It works out the five daily prayer times (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha) for where you are, and at each one your phone rings like an incoming call until you answer. People use it as a salah, salat or namaz alarm that's hard to sleep through.

      It also has morning, evening and before-sleep adhkār with a checklist, 39 duʿās with the Arabic, transliteration and meaning, the full adhan, a qibla compass, an Islamic calendar, and widgets. It has no ads, no account and no tracking, and it's in 9 languages.

      How is Hayya different from other prayer time apps?

      Most prayer time apps play the adhan as a notification you can swipe away. Hayya rings like a phone call, full screen, and calls again a few minutes later until you answer or the time for that prayer ends. Answering logs the prayer. Declining asks you why. Missing one breaks your streak.

      If you'd rather not be rung, you can switch any prayer to a quiet notification instead.

      How does Hayya compare with Muslim Pro, Athan and Pillars?

      Hayya is free with no ads and no in-app purchases, and its App Store privacy label lists no data used to track you. Muslim Pro and Athan are free with ads and sell paid plans; Pillars has no ads and sells a Premium plan, which is what unlocks its Apple Watch app. The biggest difference is the alert: the others notify you with the adhan, while Hayya rings like a phone call and calls again until you answer. Hayya has no Quran reader or mosque finder, which Muslim Pro and Athan do. See the full comparison.

      Which prayer time calculation methods does it use?

      12 of them: Muslim World League, ISNA (North America), Umm al-Qura (Makkah), Egyptian General Authority, University of Karachi, Dubai, Kuwait, Qatar, Singapore, Turkey (Diyanet), Tehran and the Moonsighting Committee. Asr can be standard (Shafiʿi, Maliki, Hanbali) or Hanafi. Hayya picks the usual method for your country, and you can change it.

      Is Hayya free?

      Yes. It's free to download, with no in-app purchases, no subscription and no ads.

      What do I need to run it?

      An iPhone on iOS 16.4 or later, or an Android phone on Android 8 or later.

      For the watch apps: an Apple Watch on watchOS 10 or later, or a Wear OS watch on Wear OS 3 or later. The Hayya watch face needs Wear OS 6 or later.

      Is there a watch app?

      Yes, for Apple Watch and for Wear OS watches like the Galaxy Watch and Pixel Watch. Both show today's times and count down to the next prayer on your watch face. On Wear OS the call shows on your watch too: you can snooze it there, and Answer or Decline opens Hayya on your phone. See the watch screens.

      Is there an Islamic watch face with Arabic numerals?

      Yes. Hayya's Wear OS watch face comes in 5 styles: Arabic numerals (Eastern Arabic digits), an eight-pointed star, classic, analog and digital, each in 12 colours. The next prayer and a countdown to it are always on the dial. It needs Wear OS 6 or later, on watches like the Galaxy Watch and Pixel Watch. Try the styles and colours.

      Does it remind me to say my morning and evening adhkar?

      Yes. Turn on daily adhkar reminders in Settings for morning, evening and before sleep, at the times you choose, and after-prayer reminders for the adhkar after each salah. Each list is a checklist: tap a card when you've said it, or tap to count for dhikr said 33 times. Finish the list and confetti falls. Try the checklist.

      Which languages is it in?

      English, Arabic, Indonesian, Bengali, Urdu, Turkish, Malay, Persian and French. Pick yours in Settings.

      Does it need internet?

      No. Prayer times are worked out on your phone from your coordinates, so everything works offline. You only need a connection to look up a city by name, or to stream Surat al-Kahf.

      Why does it ask for my location?

      Prayer times depend on where the sun is, which depends on where you are. Hayya takes one reading and works out the times on your phone. The coordinates are never sent anywhere. You can skip the permission and type your city instead.

      Will the alarm wake me for Fajr?

      That's what it's built for. Alarms are exact and are set again after a restart. On Android, Hayya walks you through the two system settings that stop your phone delaying alarms: "Alarms & reminders" access and a battery-optimization exemption.

      Can I try the alarm without waiting for a prayer?

      Yes. In Alarms, under the call theme, press Preview, or try a call as soon as you pick a theme. It rings the full call, exactly as it will at prayer time, and doesn't touch your log or streak. You can also try it on this page.

      Do I have to be rung? It's a lot.

      No. In Alarms, then How prayers arrive, switch to Notification only. Every prayer then comes as one quiet banner you can tap when you're ready: no ringing, no calling back, and nothing logged as missed unless you say so.

      Can I pause it during my period?

      Yes. Turn on the period pause in Settings. There are no prayer calls or reminders while it's on, and your streak stays as it is.

      Can I set my own alarms, like Tahajjud?

      Yes. Alongside the five prayers you can add your own, at a clock time or tied to a prayer, like "20 minutes before Fajr" or "45 minutes after Maghrib". They can repeat daily, on chosen days, or ring once. Answering one never touches your prayer log.

      What happens if I miss one?

      If a prayer's time ends without an answer, it's logged as missed and your streak starts again. If you did pray and forgot to log it, tap the prayer: once for prayed, twice for prayed late.
